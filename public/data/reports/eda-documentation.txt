E-Commerce Sales Analysis Report (Nigeria)

1. Executive Summary
The analysis was performed on a Nigerian e-commerce dataset comprising. The objective is to understand revenue drivers, customer behavior, and regional performance to help in supporting data-driven business decisions.
Overall, the analysis reveals a stable and evenly distributed market, with consistent customer spending patterns and minimal variability across regions. Revenue is primarily driven by order volume rather than differences in customer spending.

2. Key Metrics
•	Total Revenue: ₦7,612,394,701
•	Total Orders: 29,811
•	Average Order Value: ₦255,355
•	Median Order Value: ₦257,350
•	Average Shipping Fee: ₦3,002
•	Maximum Transaction Value: ₦504,529

3. Revenue and Regional Performance
Revenue is relatively evenly distributed across regions, with no extreme outliers.
•	Top-performing cities such as Aba, Calabar, and Warri generate revenues exceeding ₦510 million
•	Lower-performing cities like Onitsha and Kano generate approximately ₦480M–₦495M
This suggests:
•	A well-distributed customer base
•	No over-reliance on a single geographic market
 
 



4. Customer Spending Behavior
Customer spending patterns are highly consistent:
•	Mean Order Value: ₦252,353
•	Median Order Value: ₦254,192
•	Standard Deviation: ₦143,122
•	Range: ₦5,030 – ₦499,979
Key observations:
•	The mean and median are very close, indicating stable and predictable spending behavior
•	Skewness ≈ 0 (-0.0096) shows a nearly perfect symmetrical distribution
•	Customers generally spend within a consistent range.

5. Transaction Value Analysis
•	Mean Total Fee: ₦255,355
•	Median: ₦257,350
•	Maximum: ₦504,529
Most transactions cluster around the ₦250K range, indicating:
•	A standard purchasing pattern
•	High-value transactions exist but do not dominate revenue

6. Shipping Fee Analysis
•	Average Shipping Fee: ₦3,002
•	Range: ₦1,000 – ₦5,000
•	Standard Deviation: ₦1,155
Shipping fees represent approximately 1.2% of total order value, indicating:
•	Minimal financial impact on purchasing decisions
•	Likely standardized pricing across regions

7. Distribution Characteristics
•	Kurtosis ≈ -1.2 (flat distribution)
•	Skewness ≈ 0 (symmetrical distribution)
These metrics indicate:
•	A balanced dataset with no heavy concentration of extreme values
•	Revenue is not driven by a small number of unusually large transactions

8. Platform Performance
Major platforms such as Jumia, Konga, and Jiji dominate revenue generation, while smaller platforms contribute less significantly.
This reflects:
•	A concentrated competitive landscape
•	Strong market positioning of leading platforms

 

9. Customer Distribution
Customer activity is broadly distributed across regions:
•	Total orders: 29,811
•	No single region dominates significantly
This reinforces the presence of a diverse and geographically spread customer base

 

10. Key Insights
•	Revenue is evenly distributed across regions, indicating a balanced and diversified market
•	Customer spending is highly consistent, with most transactions centered around ₦250K–₦260K
•	Regional performance differences are driven primarily by order volume rather than spending behavior
•	Shipping fees are negligible relative to transaction value, and unlikely to influence customer decisions
•	The market is dominated by a few major platforms, suggesting competitive concentration

11. Recommendations
•	Focus growth strategies on high-performing regions (Aba, Calabar, Warri) to maximize returns
•	Increase order volume in lower-performing regions such as Onitsha and Kano through targeted marketing
•	Invest further in top-performing platforms (Jumia, Konga, Jiji) to leverage their large customer base.
•	Revenue generated by different platforms, regions, etc. were more centered on the volume of purchases rather that pricing of commodity. Run campaigns aimed at increasing purchasing frequency rather than order value.
